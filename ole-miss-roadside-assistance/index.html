Dead battery after sitting all week

This is the single most common call we get from students. A car that sits parked in a dorm lot or student housing spot for days at a time slowly drains its battery, especially if it's already a couple years old. Game Days

Home games mean heavier traffic around campus

On Ole Miss home game days, the roads around Vaught-Hemingway Stadium and the Grove get crowded fast, and that slows everyone down — including us. If you're dealing with a dead battery or an empty tank on a game day, call as early as possible and give us a specific lot, deck, or landmark. We'll get to you as fast as the traffic allows and keep you updated. Read more on our blog:game day breakdowns — parking, traffic, and getting help in Oxford.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you need a parking permit or pass to reach me on campus?

No — we don't need a permit to come help you. Just tell us which lot or deck you're in and a nearby landmark so we can find you quickly.

Can you get to me during a home football game?

We'll do our best, but game days mean heavy foot and car traffic around campus and the Grove, so response times run longer than a normal weekday. Call as early as you can and give us a specific location — the sooner we know, the better we can plan around road closures.

My car sat all week and won't start now — is that normal?

Very common. Batteries slowly lose charge even when a car isn't being driven, and older batteries lose it faster. If this keeps happening every time you leave town, your battery is probably close to needing replacement — we'll let you know what we see when we jump it.

Do you cover dorm parking lots and student housing off campus?

Yes, both. Campus dorm lots and off-campus student housing around Molly Barr Rd and Old Taylor Rd are regular calls for us.
